HR ID Platform — what to improve

A verified review of the local-first employee registry and bilingual ID-card issuance monorepo, weighted toward missing features and user-experience quality. Every finding below was confirmed by reading the source; the highest-severity claims were re-checked independently.

3 September 2026 ~57,000 lines TypeScript 3 apps · 8 packages 100 findings

The short version

The synchronous core of this product is real and often well built. Auth, RBAC, tenant scoping, optimistic concurrency, immutable print snapshots, the import wizard, backup envelope crypto and the hardened zip extractor are all genuine, careful engineering — better than most codebases this age.

Almost everything asynchronous, scheduled, or machine-readable is a facade. The worker does nothing but log a heartbeat. The JobQueue table has lease and retry columns and zero references. Print jobs never leave QUEUED. Scheduled backups stamp lastRunAt and produce no backup. Cards never expire. Both the phone-handoff QR and the printed card barcode are decorative icons.

The most dangerous findings are the ones where the UI actively reports success: the backup schedule's "last run", the print queue depth, worker health, the TLS certificate download, and the LAN toggle. An operator has no way to discover they are being told something untrue.

Separately, docs/status.md declares "Release Gate: READY (v1.0.0-rc.1)" with all 13 phases complete. That claim does not survive contact with the source — and four independent defects mean a clean deployment cannot currently start at all (findings 4, 5, 6, 7).
